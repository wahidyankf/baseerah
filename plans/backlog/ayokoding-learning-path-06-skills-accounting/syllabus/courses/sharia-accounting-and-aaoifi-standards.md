# Sharia Accounting and AAOIFI Standards (Annotated-concept)

**Course ID**: `sharia-accounting-and-aaoifi-standards` · **Format**: Annotated-concept. Sharia-only
(`sharia-accounting` manifest).

**Short summary**: The landscape of Sharia-compliant accounting — AAOIFI's standards, the
three-jurisdiction adoption picture, and the doctrinal principles that make Sharia accounting a
distinct discipline rather than conventional accounting with different labels.

**Scope note**: a landscape and judgment framework — the doctrinal foundations and standards ecosystem
— not yet the contract-by-contract mechanics (#21's subject). The entry point to Stage 3, gated on
[OI-2](../../verification-log.md) remaining explicitly `OPEN` — this course states the practical
consequence of the riba prohibition without claiming the doctrinal debate is settled.

## Why this exists · the big idea

- **The problem before the solution**: a reader arriving from Stage 2's conventional-accounting spine
  might assume "Sharia accounting" means the same mechanics with an Islamic-finance vocabulary layered
  on top. It does not — the underlying transaction structures are genuinely different (a murabaha is a
  trade, not a disguised loan), and treating them as conventional-accounting-in-translation produces
  systems that are compliant in name only.
- **Keep-this-if-you-forget-everything**: Sharia-compliant finance requires profit to arise from trade,
  leasing, partnership, or service risk — never from a predetermined return on a pure loan. This
  practical consequence is well-attested; the underlying doctrinal debate about _why_ (including a
  minority time-value-of-money position) is not this corpus's to settle.
- **Big ideas touched**: `form-vs-substance` — Sharia compliance is determined by a contract's actual
  economic substance, not its label — and `standard-plurality`, extended a third time: AAOIFI, PSAK
  Syariah, and conventional IFRS/GAAP are three standards ecosystems, not one with regional dialects.

## Concepts

- **co-01 · sharia-compliance-as-substance-test** — compliance is determined by a contract's actual
  economic substance (risk-sharing, asset-backing, real trade), not by its label or documentation.
- **co-02 · riba-prohibition-practical-consequence** — profit must arise from trade, leasing,
  partnership, or service risk, never a predetermined return on a pure loan (see the OI-2 caveat
  above for what is and is not settled here).
- **co-03 · gharar-prohibition** — excessive uncertainty or ambiguity in a contract's terms is
  prohibited, distinct from ordinary commercial risk, which is permitted.
- **co-04 · asset-backing-requirement** — many Sharia-compliant instruments require a real underlying
  asset or trade, not a purely financial claim.
- **co-05 · profit-loss-sharing-principle** — partnership-based instruments (Mudaraba, Musharaka)
  share both profit and loss between parties, rather than guaranteeing a fixed return to one party.
- **co-06 · aaoifi-standards-ecosystem** — AAOIFI issues Financial Accounting Standards (FAS) and
  Shari'ah Standards specific to Islamic financial contracts; the `[Verified]` FAS numbers above are
  this corpus's anchor points.
- **co-07 · psak-syariah-ecosystem** — Indonesia's own PSAK Syariah series (101–110), a
  jurisdiction-specific standards set distinct from AAOIFI, used alongside it as Indonesia's basis.
- **co-08 · three-jurisdiction-adoption-landscape** — Indonesia, Malaysia, and the GCC states each
  relate to AAOIFI differently — a systems builder must model jurisdiction as a first-class dimension
  for Sharia-compliant reporting, exactly as #14 argued for IFRS-vs-GAAP.
- **co-09 · sharia-supervisory-board** — a Sharia-compliant institution's governance layer that
  reviews and approves products and transactions for compliance, distinct from a conventional audit
  committee.

## Tensions & trade-offs — what this course does and does not settle

- **Practical consequence vs. doctrinal debate**: this course and this corpus teach the practical
  accounting consequence of Sharia principles (co-02 through co-05) because that consequence is
  well-attested and actionable for a systems builder. They do not adjudicate the underlying juristic
  debate — that is explicitly out of scope, and OI-2's OPEN status reflects that boundary honestly
  rather than papering over it.
- **One jurisdiction's practice is not a universal rule**: a pattern observed in one AAOIFI-adopting
  jurisdiction should not be assumed to generalise to a non-adopting one (co-08) — the divergence is
  the point, not an inconvenience to abstract away.

## Worked examples

Grouped by theme; no fixed Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced bands (Annotated-concept). Every example
cites the `co-NN` it exercises.

### Theme A · Substance over form

- **ex-01 · classify-a-contract-by-substance** — given two contracts with similar-looking payment
  schedules, one a trade with a markup and one a disguised interest-bearing loan, classify each by
  substance — verify the classification rests on risk and asset-backing, not on the payment schedule's
  shape. (co-01, co-02, `form-vs-substance`)
- **ex-02 · identify-gharar-in-a-contract** — identify excessive uncertainty in a contract term
  (undefined price, unspecified delivery) versus ordinary commercial risk (a market-price fluctuation)
  — verify the distinction. (co-03)

### Theme B · The standards landscape

- **ex-03 · map-a-contract-type-to-its-fas** — map Mudaraba, Musharaka, and Murabaha to their
  corresponding `[Verified]` AAOIFI FAS numbers (FAS 3, FAS 4, FAS 28) — verify each mapping. (co-06)
- **ex-04 · compare-jurisdiction-adoption** — for Indonesia and Malaysia, state each jurisdiction's
  relationship to AAOIFI per the `[Verified, OI-3 resolved]` fact above — verify the two differ, and
  that neither is described as "AAOIFI-mandated" without qualification. (co-08)

### Theme C · What is settled and what is not

- **ex-05 · state-the-riba-consequence-without-overclaiming** — state the practical riba-prohibition
  consequence (co-02) as this course states it, and separately identify what OI-2 leaves unsettled (the
  doctrinal basis and the minority time-value-of-money position) — verify neither is presented as
  resolved. (co-02, OI-2)

### Theme D · The silent failure — a conventional model applied to an Islamic contract

- **ex-06 · accrue-a-deferred-payment-sale-like-a-loan** — account for a deferred-payment sale carrying
  an agreed markup by accruing a periodic charge on the balance outstanding, exactly as a conventional
  interest-bearing loan would be accrued, instead of carrying the agreed trade markup across the
  deferred-payment term — verify that every entry balances, that the receivable still amortises to zero
  on schedule, and that the total recognised over the full term can be made to agree with the
  contract's own total, while each period's reported figure nonetheless describes the transaction as a
  return on money outstanding rather than as profit on a trade. Name the check that would surface it:
  co-01's substance test applied to the accounting entries themselves, not only to the contract
  document. This is a presentation and classification failure — it settles nothing about OI-2's open
  doctrinal question, which this example must not be read as answering. (co-01, co-02, co-06,
  silent-failure)

## Applied synthesis (no build — A6)

Take one real-looking contract description and classify it as Sharia-compliant or not by substance
(risk-sharing, asset-backing, absence of a predetermined loan return), map it to its corresponding
AAOIFI FAS number, and state which jurisdiction's adoption relationship would apply if the counterparty
were based in Indonesia versus Malaysia. Verify the classification, the FAS mapping, and the
jurisdiction comparison each rest on the `[Verified]` facts above, not on OI-2's open doctrinal
question. No system is built — the synthesis is the hand-worked classification and mapping.
